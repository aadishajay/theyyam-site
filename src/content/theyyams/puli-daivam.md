## Who is Puli Daivam?

Puli Daivam, the tiger deities, are a family of Theyyams of North Malabar. “Puli” means tiger. Kerala Tourism lists several of them: Puliyoor Kali, Pulikarinkali, Pulikandan, Marapuli, Kandapuli and Pulimaruthan.

## The story

The stories say that Shiva and Parvati, passing through a forest, saw tigers and took the forms of a tiger, Pulikandan, and a tigress, Pulikarinkali. The tigress gave birth in a cave beneath a rock, and the cubs became tiger deities. The best known is a daughter, Puliyoor Kali. The sons are named variously, and the number of children differs between accounts.

Puliyoor Kali often appears alone as a companion deity in a shrine. When she appears with her mother, the mother is called Valiya Thamburatti and the daughter Cheriya Thamburatti. In some places she appears together with Puliyoor Kannan. These details come mainly from popular sources, so please treat them as one tradition among several.

## What you will see

Puliyoor Kali wears a very tall, round headdress, an orange-red painted face and richly layered red attire. She is described as fierce, with angry postures and graceful steps and spins, and at one point the sacred headdress is lowered to touch the ground in reverence.

::gloss

## In the tradition

Puliyoor Kali is kept by the Vannan community. The tiger deities are understood as protectors, and are approached with respect.

:::note
Pulimaranja Thondachan, who is described in a separate page, is a different tiger deity with a different story.
:::
