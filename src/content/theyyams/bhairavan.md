## Who is Bhairavan?

Bhairavan is the fierce, wandering form of Shiva, who is shown as an ascetic. He is the chief deity of the Paanan community, and members of the Malayan community also keep him. Folklore writers place him among the deities that need especially careful preparation.

## The story

One story links him to Shiva as Kapaladhari, the skull-bearer. After Shiva cut off the fifth head of Brahma, he was cursed to carry the skull and beg. The paradox of a beggar who is also the ruler of the cosmos is central to the Theyyam. The Paanan tradition draws on the Tamil text Periya Puranam, though we have not found which episodes.

::gallery

## What you will see

The face is painted with natural colours in a fierce pattern, and the attire is rich and red, with a very tall headdress. The ritual is usually at night and can end in the early hours.

::gloss

## In the tradition

The preparation takes many hours and includes an invocation song, face painting, dressing and the placing of the headdress, which is understood as the moment when the deity arrives.

:::note
Bhairavan is a fierce form. Keep your voice low and give the koladhari space.
:::
