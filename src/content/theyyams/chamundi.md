## Who is Chamundi?

Chamundi is the fierce, protective form of the goddess, worshipped in several local forms. Three well-known types are Madayil Chamundi, Rakta Chamundi and Kundora Chamundi. Chamundi shrines are often kept by ruling families and households alongside those of Kurathi and Vishnumoorthi.

## The story

In the old stories, Chamundi is identified with Kali, who killed the demons Chanda and Munda and drank the blood of Raktabija so that none reached the ground. In one form, she followed her enemies into the underworld, which is why she is called Madayil Chamundi.

Kundora Chamundi is understood as a fierce form of Kali who defeated the demon Darika. She is honoured as a village guardian and a household deity, and is traditionally kept by the Velan community.

## What you will see

Many forms of Chamundi have a very large, round, halo-like headdress edged with palm leaf and decorated in red, with a fierce painted face and rich ornaments.

::gloss

## In the tradition

As a warrior goddess she is a figure of both protection and fear, and rituals are shaped by older traditions of sacrifice and offering.

:::note
Chamundi’s large round headdress is heavy and wide. Keep well clear when the Theyyam turns.
:::
