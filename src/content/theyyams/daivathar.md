## Who is Daivathar?

Daivathar is one of the two major Vaishnava deities in Theyyam, with Vishnumoorthi. Scholars note that Vaishnavism arrived late in Kerala’s folk religion, so there are few Theyyams in this group. Kerala Tourism says Daivathar stands for Rama, and describes it as part of a set of Ramayana-based Theyyams, with Angakkaran and Bappuran.

## The story

We did not find a published origin story specific to Daivathar. In the Tulu-speaking region, Vaishnavism was popular from about the thirteenth century, and one scholarly view is that Vishnu-linked deities were taken into the older spirit-worship of the coast before entering Theyyam.

## What you will see

In photographs, Daivathar wears a tall, pointed golden crown and red attire, with an orange-painted face.

::gloss

## In the tradition

Daivathar belongs to the small Vaishnava group of Theyyams, alongside Vishnumoorthi, in a tradition where most forms are Shaiva or Shakta.

Ask your guide how this Theyyam is held at the shrine you visit.
