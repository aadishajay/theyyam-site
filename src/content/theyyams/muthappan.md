## Who is Muthappan?

Muthappan means “grandfather” in Malayalam, and that is how he is approached: as a close, forgiving elder who is easy to talk to. He is worshipped as two forms of one deity, Thiruvappana and Vellatom, and is often linked with Shiva. His rites are traditionally led by the Thiyya community, who regard him as their group ancestor.

## The story

A well-known story says that a childless nobleman and his wife, devotees of Shiva, found a beautiful baby boy on a rock and raised him as their son. As he grew he took food to poor families and mixed freely with everyone, which went against the customs of the household. When he revealed that he was divine, he left home. Because his gaze burned whatever it touched, he is said to have pierced his own eyes to spare the world, which is why one form is shown as blind.

On his journey he was drawn to the toddy palms of a village. A toddy tapper who tried to catch him stealing found himself unable to move, and his wife recognised the old man in the palm tree and called him “Muthappan”. She offered him boiled gram, coconut slices, grilled fish and toddy, and he made the place his home.

## What you will see

Muthappan appears with a painted face and a tall headdress, and a calm, kindly manner. The Theyyam sits and talks with devotees, listens to their troubles and gives blessings. Dogs are closely associated with him.

::gloss

## In the tradition

The shrines, called madappuras, have no idol and no flagstaff. Offerings are distinctive, including boiled gram, coconut and, in some practice, toddy and fish. Entry is not limited by caste or religion, and people of many communities take part. Unlike most Theyyam, Muthappan is observed through the year, not only in the main season.

:::note
Muthappan is a particularly personal deity. Visitors should expect devotees to approach him with quiet affection and should take their cue from the people around them.
:::
