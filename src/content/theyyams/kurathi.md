## Who is Kurathi Amma?

Kurathi Amma is a much-loved goddess of North Malabar, worshipped in family homes, village groves and temples. “Kurathi” is a word for a woman of the hill people, and she is often understood as a form of Parvati, the daughter of the mountain. Scholars note that goddesses such as Kurathi and Chamundi are especially honoured in the household shrines of ruling families.

## The story

Tradition describes her as a guardian of the family and the home who guards against harm of many kinds. Women have a particular devotion to her, and they often prepare food to be offered to her.

In a story told about a related goddess, a farmer’s sister dies after eating betel leaf from a plot that Kurathi had been asked to guard. When the farmer cries out, Kurathi draws coal from the funeral pyre with her sword, and a new goddess rises from it. This is a local telling and differs from place to place.

## What you will see

Kurathi Amma wears a tall red headdress with round ornaments on either side, a bright orange-red painted face and a skirt of palm-leaf strips. Her bearing is dignified and watchful.

::gloss

## In the tradition

She is reportedly the first Theyyam to appear at many shrines. Devotees ask for protection and for the wellbeing of the family and its fields.

:::note
Many women come forward to speak to Kurathi Amma. Give them space, and keep your voice low.
:::
