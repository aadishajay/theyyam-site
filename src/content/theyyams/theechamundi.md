## Who is Theechamundi?

Theechamundi is a fire-linked Theyyam that is connected in most accounts with Vishnu in his Narasimha form. Although the name carries “Chamundi”, which is usually a goddess, the tellings we have found centre on Vishnu and fire. Accounts differ from shrine to shrine, so please treat this as a general summary. It is also known as Ottakolam, a name used for a Theyyam that appears alone.

## The story

One telling links it to the story of the child devotee Prahlada, whom his father, the demon king Hiranyakashipu, tried to destroy by fire, among other trials. Vishnu protected the boy and appeared as Narasimha, the half-man, half-lion.

Another telling says that after Narasimha’s victory the worlds rejoiced, but Agni, the god of fire, did not join in and made light of the deed. Vishnu, angered, entered the sacrificial fire and subdued Agni. A third account says Shiva created a fire from his third eye, which Narasimha entered to cool his rage. All the versions are oral tradition and have no single written source.

## What you will see

The koladhari wears attire of palm-leaf strips and a painted face, with a decorated headdress. The ritual centres on fire, and it is one of the most intense of all Theyyam rituals.

::gloss

## In the tradition

The deity is approached with devotion and fear, and it is understood as an expression of the god’s protective power rather than a display. As with other Theyyams, devotees may receive a blessing afterwards.

:::note
This is a high-risk ritual. Always keep to the boundary set by the organisers and your guide, and never move closer to the fire for a better view.
:::
