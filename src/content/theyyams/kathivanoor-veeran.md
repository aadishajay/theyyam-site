## Who is Kathivanoor Veeran?

Kathivanoor Veeran is one of the most popular Theyyams of North Malabar. “Veeran” means hero. He is remembered as Mandappan Chekavar, a Thiyya warrior whose life and death are still told in the folklore of the Kolathunadu region.

## The story

Mandappan was born with the blessing of the goddess Chuzali and grew up skilled in martial arts. He preferred hunting in the woods to working, and his father, angry, broke his bow. Mandappan left home, went to live with his uncle, did well in business and married Chemmarathi.

When news came that an army from Kodagu was about to attack, he took up arms and won the battle. On the way home he realised he had lost a ring and a little finger and went back for them. Enemy soldiers killed him by treachery. Chemmarathi, on seeing the ring and finger, joined his funeral pyre. Afterwards, the two were seen as divine, and his uncle gave the Theyyam its name, Kathivanoor Veeran.

## What you will see

Kathivanoor Veeran is known for dynamic, flexible movement. The face carries a beard and moustache in a style called nakam thazhthi ezhuthu. The Theyyam is traditionally held at night or in the early morning. A raised structure made of banana stems, called Chemmarathi thara, represents his wife. Its sixty-four cells recall that his body was said to have been cut into sixty-four pieces.

::gloss

## In the tradition

Many young women of North Malabar pray to Kathivanoor Veeran for a healthy husband. The koladhari is expected to know a great deal, as visitors may put questions to the deity and are given answers.

:::note
Kathivanoor Veeran’s ritual involves a lot of fire and movement. Follow your guide’s advice on where to stand.
:::
