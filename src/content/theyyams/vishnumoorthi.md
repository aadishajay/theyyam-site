## Who is Vishnumoorthi?

Vishnumoorthi is one of the two main Vaishnava deities in Theyyam. Scholars note that Vaishnavism arrived late in Kerala’s folk worship, so Vishnumoorthi stands out in a tradition mostly shaped by Shaiva and Shakta forms. The deity is also called Narasimhamoorthi because the ritual retells Vishnu’s avatar as Narasimha, the half-man, half-lion.

## The story

The ritual remembers Palanthai Kannan, a devoted follower of Vishnu. As a boy he was beaten and driven out of his village for picking mangoes from a landlord’s tree. He found refuge in a Vishnu shrine on the coast, and after many years he was told to return home. On his return he was killed. Vishnu, who had accompanied his devotee, destroyed the landlord’s house in anger.

An astrologer then told the landlord that the god was displeased and ordered a shrine and a ritual to be made for him. From then on the god was worshipped as Vishnumoorthi. A second reading of the Theyyam sees it as Narasimha emerging from a pillar to protect the child Prahlada from his father, the demon king Hiranyakashipu.

## What you will see

The deity wears a tall, red headdress, heavy red attire and a long skirt of palm-leaf strips. The drumming is loud and unmistakable, and the movements are strong and sudden. Many devotees say the Narasimha part of the ritual is among the most intense in North Malabar.

::gloss

## In the tradition

The Theyyam is held at many shrines and family homes. In some forms the koladhari goes through a rite with fire, which is part of the deity’s tradition, and the Theyyam is traditionally kept by members of the Malaya community.

:::note
Because the movements are fast and the crowd presses in, a guide will place you where you can see safely. Stand back when asked.
:::
