## Why Thalassery

Thalassery is an old harbour town on the Malabar coast. For centuries it was a spice port, and in the 1700s it became a British trading post, which is why the old name Tellicherry still turns up on maps and in books. Many people know it first for its food, its sport and its sea-front streets. Fewer know that it is also close to some of the busiest Theyyam country in Kerala.

That is the reason we use it as a base. A Theyyam is a ritual held at night, often until the early hours. The days before and after are for rest, and Thalassery gives you plenty to do with them.

## Cake

Thalassery's best-known food story is the Christmas cake. The town is credited with baking India's first Christmas plum cake. The commonly told version is that a Scottish planter, Murdoch Brown, shared an English-style cake with a local baker, Mambally Bapu, who then learned to bake it himself. The year most often given is 1883, though sources differ, and some give 1884 or give a different date for the bakery's founding.

Whatever the exact year, the bakery tradition is real and still alive in town. Thalassery is also known for its biscuits and for its biryani, which is cooked in a style of its own. If you only eat one thing, ask your host where they buy their bread.

## Circus

Thalassery is often called the birthplace of the Indian circus. The name usually linked with this is Keeleri Kunhikannan, born here in 1858 and remembered as the father of the Indian circus. He is said to have trained gymnasts and acrobats in the town, helped by the region's older kalari tradition of martial training, and some of his students went on to start circus companies of their own.

You will not find a circus ring on the map for visitors, but you will find the history in the town's talk, and in the families who still teach it.

## Cricket

Cricket in Thalassery is older than most people expect. Local tradition links it to the early 1800s and to Arthur Wellesley, later the Duke of Wellington, who is said to have played here and to have filled his teams with local men when he was short of players. The story is told widely, but it comes more from tradition than from firm records, and the dates vary between sources.

What is clear is that the town has a long cricket habit. Its Municipal Stadium is described by Kerala Tourism as dating from 1802, and Kerala is reported to have won its first Ranji Trophy match on this ground, in 1963. Grounds change and are renovated, so ask locally before planning to watch a match.

## Theyyam around Thalassery

Theyyam is not a visitor attraction in Thalassery. It is a ritual, held for a deity who is believed to be present, usually by a family or a community at its own shrine. During the season, from late October to May, rituals take place across the Thalassery area, in village shrines called *kavus* and in family homes.

Which ritual is held where, and whether visitors are welcome, is decided locally and often confirmed only days before. That is why we do not publish dates, and why a host or guide who knows the family makes such a difference. Read [Theyyam etiquette](theyyam-etiquette.html) before you go, and [Theyyam season](theyyam-season.html) to choose your months.

## A day around the ritual

On the days of a trip based in Thalassery, we usually fit in a few of the town's own sights, because the ritual night needs a slow day either side:

- **Sea-front and old town:** Thalassery Fort and the sea-front garden at Overbury's Folly, with its sea view, are an easy morning. Sources differ on when each was built.
- **Muzhappilangad Beach:** described by Kerala Tourism as the longest drive-in beach in Asia, and sheltered enough for a swim. Dharmadam Island sits just off the shore.
- **The Gundert Bungalow:** the home of the German scholar Hermann Gundert, who prepared the first Malayalam dictionary.
- **Food:** a bakery stop and a biryani lunch.

Then rest, and the evening belongs to the ritual.
