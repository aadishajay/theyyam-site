Theyyam season: the best months to visit North Malabar

By [AUTHOR NAME] · Updated [DATE] · LGFC local guides

The Theyyam season in North Malabar runs from late October to May. The ritual year follows temple and family calendars rather than tourist ones, so each part of the season has a different feel.

The short answer

Late October to May. Rituals are not spread evenly across those months, and which shrines hold them in a given week is decided locally. That is why we confirm the right nights with you closer to travel.

Late October to December

The season opens as the monsoon fades. The land is green, the air is humid and showers are still possible. It is a quieter start and a good time for a first visit.

January to February

The coolest and driest months, with comfortable evenings for late nights outdoors. This is the most popular stretch for visitors, so stays and drivers book early.

March to May

Hotter and more humid, with rituals running into the pre-monsoon heat. The crowds are thinner. Plan your days around rest, shade and water.

How to choose

Tell us your travel month, how long you have and whether you are travelling with children or elders. We will suggest the right base town and pace. Start an enquiry.

Quick answers

When is the Theyyam season?

Late October to May. The best weeks depend on where you want to base yourself, and we confirm them with you when you enquire.

Is there Theyyam in the monsoon?

The main season is outside the monsoon. Rituals are rare in the heavy rains of June to September.

Which months are most comfortable?

January and February are the coolest and driest. March to May is hotter.
