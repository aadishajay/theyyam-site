What is Theyyam? A first-timer’s guide to Kerala’s living ritual

By [AUTHOR NAME] · Updated [DATE] · LGFC local guides

Theyyam is a ritual in which a performer, after hours of face painting and dressing, is believed to become a deity. It is performed in family shrines and village grounds across north Kerala, mostly between late October and May.

What Theyyam is

The word comes from daivam, meaning god. Each Theyyam is a particular deity with its own story, costume, face paint and song. Some are gods, some are heroes, ancestors or spirits. Hundreds of forms exist.

It is worship, not a staged show. Families and villages gather because the deity is present, and the performer blesses, answers questions and settles disputes.

Who performs it

Performers belong to specific families and communities who have held the tradition for generations. Training begins in childhood, learning songs, steps and the craft of costume.

“The performer is not acting. For the length of the ritual, the village speaks to the god.”

Preparation can take several hours before the deity appears.

What you will see

Drums and a song of invocation begin the ritual. The performer wears a towering headdress, painted face and layered costume. Some forms are fierce and some gentle; each has its own song, gestures and story.

How to be a good guest

Dress modestly, keep your voice low, never step into the ritual space unless invited, and remember that photography and video are banned at some rituals. Always ask your guide first.

Quick answers

When is the season?

Late October to May, with exact timings confirmed locally.

Is it safe for visitors?

Yes, with a guide. Crowds, heat and late nights are the main things to plan for.

Do I need to book?

Many rituals are open, but a guided trip removes the guesswork. See guided trips.
